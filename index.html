Abstract

Humans rely on vision and touch to manipulate everyday objects, using tactile feedback to determine how an interaction should proceed. When scooping grains, feeling the handle’s shape helps us infer spoon orientation, while resistance transmitted through the grasp guides our motion. These decisions depend on two essential aspects of touch: detailed contact geometry and interaction shear forces.

To bring these sensing capabilities to robots, we present PanoTac, a low-cost, compliant tactile fingertip designed for dense three-axis tactile sensing. Each finger provides a 12 × 32 taxel grid, with each taxel representing three components: normal (z) and shear (x, y). Sensor characterization demonstrates spatially consistent and temporally repeatable responses across all three axes.

We integrate PanoTac into a robotic system with a visuo-tactile learning pipeline. Across test tube preparation, grain scooping, and salt pouring, the resulting policies outperform vision-only baselines by an average of 43.3%. Ablations show that removing either spatial contact information or load feedback reduces overall performance across the task suite.

The learning system

Touch as an observation stream.

Two PanoTac fingertips equip a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper on a Franka Panda arm. A U-Net diffusion policy combines wrist-camera images, the dense normal array, magnetic layer readings, and robot proprioception.

Modality-specific encoders are trained jointly with the policy, which predicts end-effector poses and gripper commands. During demonstration collection, tactile feedback is also sonified to support the teleoperator.
