PanoTac: A Dense Three-Axis Tactile Sensor for Robust Touch-Centric Manipulation

Abstract

Humans rely on vision and touch to manipulate everyday objects, using tactile feedback to determine how an interaction should proceed. When scooping grains, feeling the handle’s shape helps us infer spoon orientation, while resistance transmitted through the grasp guides our motion. These decisions depend on two essential aspects of touch: detailed contact geometry and interaction shear forces.

To bring these sensing capabilities to robots, we present PanoTac, a low-cost, compliant tactile fingertip designed for dense three-axis tactile sensing. Each finger provides a 12 × 32 taxel grid, with each taxel representing three components: normal (z) and shear (x, y). Sensor characterization demonstrates spatially consistent and temporally repeatable responses across all three axes.

We integrate PanoTac into a robotic system with a visuo-tactile learning pipeline. Across test tube preparation, grain scooping, and salt pouring, the resulting policies outperform vision-only baselines by an average of 46.7 percentage points. Ablations show that removing either spatial contact information or load feedback reduces overall performance across the task suite.

04 / Failure modes

Representative failures of the baseline policies on each task.

The robot fails to insert the test tube into the rack opening.

The robot lifts the tube before the stopper has been released.

The tube slips from the grasp as the robot lifts it too early.

The robot continues wiggling even after the stopper has been released.

The stopper falls off during wiggling rather than being removed in a controlled motion.

The spoon moves in the wrong direction during the scooping motion.

The robot does not fully pour the grain out of the spoon.

The spoon becomes unstable as the robot returns it after pouring.

The robot discards a bottle that still contains salt.

The robot fails to discard the bottle after it is empty.

The robot stops pouring before all of the salt has left the bottle.

The robot attempts to pour from an empty bottle instead of discarding it.
